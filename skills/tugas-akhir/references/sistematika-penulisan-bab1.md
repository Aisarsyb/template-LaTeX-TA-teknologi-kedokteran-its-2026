# Chapter 1 Writing Guide: Introduction

- Use this guide when writing or reviewing Chapter 1 of a Medical Technology thesis proposal or final thesis.
- Follow the subsections in the manuscript; retain Scope and Limitations if the template includes it.
- Adapt research questions and benefits to the study design, not to a company-focused example.

## Background

- Start with the healthcare context or users' needs, then narrow to the research problem.
- Describe expected and observed conditions; cite sources for figures, standards, and previous findings.
- Explain the gap left by previous research, its impact on those affected, and why this approach was chosen.
- Ground urgency in an identifiable impact and support claims that prompt attention is needed.
- **Complete when:** Readers can identify the problem, who is affected, what remains unknown or unmet, and why the study is needed.

## Research Questions

- Derive each question directly from the gap in the background.
- Specify the subject, aspect under study, and outcome to investigate, in line with the study design.
- Ask about separate and combined effects only if the study tests both.
- **Complete when:** Planned methods can answer every question using data within the study's scope.

## Scope and Limitations

- State the scope of subjects or data, testing conditions, and relevant deliberate exclusions.
- Align these limits with methods and research questions.
- **Complete when:** Readers can distinguish what the study will assess from what its findings cannot establish.

## Objectives

- Write one actionable objective per research question, in the same order.
- State what will be assessed or produced rather than results not yet obtained.
- **Complete when:** Every question has a matching objective and every objective has a corresponding question.

## Benefits

- Explain potential contributions to knowledge and practical benefits for relevant groups, such as healthcare workers, patients, healthcare facilities, or medical device developers.
- Tie each benefit to an outcome the study will measure or produce.
- Distinguish demonstrated findings from applications requiring further testing.
- **Complete when:** Claims of clinical improvement or benefits to specific groups stay within what the study design can support.
