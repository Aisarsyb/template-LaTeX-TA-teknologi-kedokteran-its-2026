# Chapter II Guide: Literature Review

Use this guide when writing or reviewing Chapter II. Follow the section order in the manuscript/template and supervisor guidance; the template places **Previous Research Findings** before **Theoretical Foundations**. Adapt subsections to the study objectives and design.

## Previous Research Findings

- Select studies that help answer the research questions or explain the choice of method, not merely studies with similar titles. Record relevant authors, year, objectives, population or data, methods, outcome measures, findings, and limitations.
- Group studies by problem, approach, or finding. Compare similarities and differences in testing conditions, outcome assessment, and limits of applicability; go beyond summarizing one article per paragraph.
- Explain what is known, what remains unanswered, and where this study fits. Identify a research gap or novelty only when the comparison of sources supports it. This section is complete when readers can see why the study is needed.

## Theoretical Foundations

- Cover concepts needed to understand the medical problem, intended users or clinical context, how the method or technology works, and the outcomes to be assessed. Move from the problem context to more specific concepts; the order of words in the title need not determine the section order.
- For each concept, explain its definition, relevant mechanism or relationship, and why it matters to this study. Explain the choice of method over relevant alternatives, including its limitations and implications for medical use.
- Define terms, variables, and outcome measures consistently with the research questions and Chapter III. Include equations or figures only when they aid explanation; explain what the symbols mean and how they relate to the study.
- This section is complete when each subsection supports the study objectives and readers understand why the method and outcome measures were chosen.

## Conceptual Framework and Hypotheses

- Connect the medical problem, previous findings, theoretical foundations, chosen method, and outcomes to be assessed in a clear line of reasoning. Use a diagram if it clarifies the relationships; explain its logic in the text.
- State hypotheses only if the study design and writing guidelines require them. Link each hypothesis to theory, previous evidence, and a testable outcome. Place this section according to the manuscript structure and supervisor guidance.

## Sources, Citations, and Review

- Prioritize relevant scholarly articles and academic books; use recent sources when the field changes rapidly, and older sources when they remain important to foundational theory. Check the original source and whether the cited page or section supports the claim.
- Paraphrase from understanding, then cite every idea, datum, or comparison drawn from a source. Use direct quotations sparingly and in line with citation rules; record source details and locations from the outset so they can be checked later.
- Before moving to Chapter III, check that all important concepts have sources; previous studies are compared rather than stacked; the reasons for choosing the method and its limits are clear; terms and outcome measures align with Chapters I and III; and the conceptual framework stays within the evidence. Flag unsupported claims for verification.
