# Chapter 5 Guide: Conclusions and Recommendations

## What sound conclusions and recommendations look like

- **Conclusions answer the research questions.** Address every research question and objective in Chapter 1 using results from Chapter 4. State what the findings mean rather than repeating the discussion or copying every number. Include key figures when needed to show the size or direction of a finding.
- **Claims match the evidence.** Distinguish supported findings from uncertain or unanswered questions. If the study tests a hypothesis, report the test result according to the method used; otherwise, draw conclusions based on the study objectives. Limit claims to the population, conditions, and outcomes studied. For example, device test results do not by themselves establish clinical benefit for patients.
- **Practical recommendations are actionable.** Name who should act, what they can do, and which finding supports the action. Recommend use in healthcare only to the extent that the study supports its safety and benefit.
- **Future research recommendations address actual limitations.** Link limitations in methods, sample, or testing to specific improvements. Propose new variables only when findings or limitations provide a reason to study them.
- **Writing is concise and scientific.** Use paragraphs for conclusions and recommendations; separate answers or recommendations only when doing so clarifies their links to the research questions.

## Practical steps for writing Chapter 5

1. **Map questions to evidence.** Read the research questions and objectives in Chapter 1. For each, locate the relevant results in Chapter 4. Mark questions that the results cannot answer. Done when every question points to a finding or is recorded as unanswered.
2. **Draft conclusions.** Write a direct answer to each question supported by results, then combine related answers into coherent paragraphs. Preserve uncertainty and limits on applying the findings. Done when every claim traces to a Chapter 4 result and stays within the study's scope.
3. **Draft practical recommendations.** Choose findings that support realistic action. State who should act, what they should do, and why the findings support it. If evidence is insufficient for use with patients, recommend further evaluation or testing instead. Done when every recommendation is grounded in a finding and does not assume untested safety or clinical benefit.
4. **Draft future research recommendations.** Check limitations documented in the methods and results. For each recommendation, state what should be improved or tested and why. Done when every recommendation addresses a documented limitation or an unanswered question.
5. **Check alignment.** Compare Chapters 1, 4, and 5. Done when every research question is answered or explicitly marked unanswered, every conclusion is supported by a result, and every recommendation has a traceable rationale.
