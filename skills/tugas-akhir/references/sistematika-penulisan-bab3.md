# Chapter III Guide: Methodology

- Use when writing or reviewing Chapter III. Follow the manuscript's section order and supervisor guidance. The template includes Research Flowchart, Materials and Equipment, System Design, Testing and Evaluation Procedures, and Activity Schedule.
- Adapt the content to the study design, including studies that do not develop a system. Write Chapter III in scientific prose; use tables or diagrams where they clarify procedures.

## Before writing

- Read the research questions and objectives in Chapter I, the rationale for the method and outcome measures in Chapter II, and the available study data.
- Identify what is studied, the data source or participants, the outcomes assessed, and limits of applicability in the medical context.
- Flag missing facts for the researcher. Distinguish planned procedures in a proposal from procedures actually performed in a final report.
- This step is complete when each objective has a testable approach that can be described without inventing data.

## Workflow, materials, and design

- Describe the sequence from data or material sources through acquisition and processing to evaluation. Ensure the flowchart matches the text.
- Specify materials, equipment, software, and specifications that affect execution or reproducibility.
- Explain inputs, work stages, outputs, key parameters, and why the chosen method fits better than relevant alternatives. Adapt the design section for studies that do not develop a system.
- For patient data, medical images, or participants: describe sources, selection criteria, sample size and its justification, required permissions or consent, and measures to protect confidentiality and data security. State approval status only when supported by evidence.

## Testing, analysis, and schedule

- For each objective, describe what is tested, any comparator or reference standard, how outcomes are measured and in what units, the testing procedure, and the analysis method.
- Explain why the outcome measures and analysis suit the claimed medical benefit, including limits of applicability.
- Use statistical tests, instrument validation, or user evaluation when the study design and data call for them.
- Fill the schedule with known activities and dates; flag uncertainties rather than copying the template's examples.

## Final check

- Ensure readers can reproduce the main steps, trace every objective to an outcome measure and analysis, understand the method choice, and identify relevant protections for participants or data.
- Align terms, stages, and metrics with Chapters I–II and with diagrams, tables, and text. Report facts that still need confirmation separately.
